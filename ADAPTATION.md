# PLEA Sepolia test run 2 adaptation

## Scope and launch compatibility

The supplied `launch.json` is retained unchanged. The factory deploys, in order and in one zero-value transaction, `PLEA`, `CabalGate`, `Seasons`, `Laureates`, `PleaDistributor`, and `PleaLaunch`. Their constructors are nonpayable and take respectively 1, 7, 3, 1, 2, and 10 static arguments. There are no externally linked libraries. Constructors access only earlier application contracts: `Seasons` reads the gate's season length, and `PleaLaunch` reads PLEA's owner and registers the system. No live oracle, token, Stacker, or PoolManager is needed for the six-contract deployment rehearsal.

Ownership resolves to the supplied owner, `0x4b91078b2374c956a65f7af0999cae0a935e6821`, rather than the factory or transaction origin. All manifest arguments, fees, prize shares, timings, panel size 30, quorum minimum 17, 18,000-second seasons, 118,800-second dead-man, `START = 1792162800`, and credit gas 1,300,000 are retained. Build configuration and vendored dependencies are unchanged, including `[lint] lint_on_build = false`.

The four requested changes were already present in the imported source and are retained:

- `src/PLEA.sol` and `src/Seasons.sol`: the one-time mint splits the fixed billion-token supply 88% / 2% / 10% among hook, Seasons, and distributor. Seasons allocates 5,000,000 PLEA to each of seasons 0–3 using the existing top-ten shares and 60-point threshold, burns unpaid shares, and burns all remaining PLEA on the final `flushToWall`. There is no bonus withdrawal or approval function. `test/SeasonFunds.t.sol` covers allocation, eligibility, failed transfers, burns, the four-season limit, and early death.
- `src/PleaHook.sol`: the daily cap allowance is fixed at seed to `(seeded inventory - 900,000e18) / 25`, with elapsed-time accounting and a remainder. The cap follows inventory down; it is not an unconditional time-based burn. `test/Hook.t.sol`, `test/AuditRevision.t.sol`, and the trading invariants cover the rate and accounting in both currency orientations.
- `src/PleaHook.sol`: the wall placement guard immediately follows lower PLEA prices and moves toward higher prices at up to 400 ticks per day, with idle time capped at one day. The wall uses the safer of spot, reference tick, and guard. `test/Hook.t.sol` and `test/Orientation.t.sol` exercise pumps, falls, and wall placement in both orientations.
- `src/CabalGate.sol` and `src/interfaces/IPleaParts.sol`: requests use Intake v2 `requestWithFailure`; the full `onPleaFailure` function and `PleaFailed` event remain. Only the configured Intake may clear a request the gate created that is still pending. Failure does not transfer or burn tokens, refund a fee, issue a verdict, or extend the Cabal's life. `expire` remains the fallback. `test/Gate.t.sol` and `test/AuditRevision.t.sol` cover failures, authorization, request ownership, replay, appeals, expiry, events, and the callback gas stipend.

The manifest retains Intake v2 `0xa43e6f75ee006411f79ac1c84120606c2330de82` and imd/acc launch #1294 Stacker `0x0457276d7b036818b3eddc1a4a3fceda61e9314d`.

- `ADAPTATION.md`: added the requested change rationale, disposition of each supplied audit finding, retained launch exceptions, and verification record.

## Changes in this revision (independent review of the accepted tree)

Each reviewer finding was reproduced on the accepted code before anything changed; the three that held are fixed in `src/PleaHook.sol`, each with a regression test in `test/Hook.t.sol` that runs in both currency orientations (`HookTest` and `HookPleaIsZeroTest`). No number changed.

1. **Wall guard adopted the empty pool's price limit — `07e31e69…c082e6` (high, fixed).** Reproduced as reported: after the Cabal's death a plain exact-in sell larger than the IMD in the pool runs to the pool's price limit, `afterSwap` observes `TickMath.MIN_TICK` (PLEA token0) or `MAX_TICK − 1` (PLEA token1), and `_updateWallGuard` moved `wallGuardTick` there at once. From then on `rebalance()` reverted `InvalidLiquidity` (the band between the guard and the tick extreme is too narrow for a `uint128` liquidity) for weeks, then parked the wall ~766k ticks from spot with the guard returning at 400 ticks a day. Fix: `_updateWallGuard` only adopts an observed tick strictly inside the usable range (`minTick < tick < maxTick`); a tick at or beyond it is where a swap stopped for lack of liquidity, not a price PLEA traded at. The guard's two speeds, `refTick`, the band geometry and `WALL_GUARD_TICKS_PER_DAY` are unchanged. `test_wallGuardIgnoresTheEmptyPoolsPriceLimit` replays the reviewer's sequence (100 IMD buy, death, a 50,000,000 PLEA sell to the limit, a 50 IMD buy an hour later, `fundWall(10e18)` twelve hours later, `rebalance()`) and checks the guard did not move on the drain and the wall's near edge is deployed within 1,000 ticks below spot; it failed on the accepted code with the guard at the extreme.
2. **Hourly checkpoint was the post-swap spot of the hour's first trade — `3280bafb…ec3c63` (medium, fixed).** Reproduced: the first buy of an hour wrote its own closing price as the hour's checkpoint, so a helper buy of ~2% lifted `latestPrice()` and the trend bucket of every plea submitted that hour (+4 points in a flat market, +9 in a falling one). Fix: `beforeSwap` saves the pool's sqrt price in transient storage (`T_PRICE_BEFORE`) and `afterSwap` checkpoints that pre-swap price, so the hour's reference is the price the first trade found, never the one it made, and no trade in an hour can move that hour's checkpoint. The ring, `latestPrice`, `price24hAgo` and the gate's buckets are unchanged; the seed checkpoint stays the initial price. `test_hourlyCheckpointIsNotSetByTheTradeThatOpensTheHour` checks the checkpoint equals the pre-trade price, that a >2% pump opening a later hour leaves `latestPrice`, `price24hAgo` and `factScore` unchanged, and that `priceX96` (spot) did move; it failed on the accepted code. Limitation stated in the README: a pump held as the last trade of the previous hour still becomes the next hour's checkpoint; while the Cabal lives only buys and gated sells move the price, so that is the same cost the design already accepts for a sustained price, not a free same-hour lever.
3. **Unlimited IMD allowance to the Stacker — `2493dd6b…c38df1e6` (low, fixed).** Reproduced: `seed()` approved the Stacker for `type(uint256).max` IMD, so a bug or compromise of that third-party contract could pull the whole wall reserve (`retainedImd`), after which `rebalance()` reverts for good. Fix: the one-time max approval is removed; `_payCashback` does `forceApprove(stacker, amount)` immediately before each `credit` (the credit consumes it) and resets it to zero when the credit reverts. The Stacker, the cashback share and the credit gas are unchanged; a buy pays about 25k more gas for the approval, inside the existing `SWAP_RESERVE`. `test_stackerIsApprovedOnlyForTheCashbackBeingCredited` checks the allowance is zero at rest, that a `transferFrom` by the Stacker against a 1,000 IMD reserve reverts, that a credit leaves no allowance, and that a reverted credit leaves none either.
4. **A 1-wei plea scores best and earns points without selling — `035b2b4a…3a60d4` (low, disputed, no change).** The behaviour reproduces as described: `_checkAmount` accepts any non-zero amount, the share bucket gives 18 points to the smallest share, and `Seasons.record` counts an approved plea that lapses. It is, however, the documented product (README: "Approved, not sold in 7 min: Lapsed; the score still counts for the season") and the brief keeps every number and the accepted behaviour. Each of the suggested remedies adds a number the brief does not give (a minimum amount or share) or changes the accepted scoring (points scaled by the executed share). The defence the design relies on is the panel: the question shows the judges the amount (a 1-wei plea reads "sell 0 PLEA") and the plea still needs a signed score of at least `need` from 30 judges at quorum 17, paid at 0.5 IMD a try. Left for the requester as an open design point; the adapter does not choose a new economic parameter.

Comparing the artifacts built immediately before and after the quorum fix confirms that both creation and runtime bytecode are identical for `PLEA`, `Seasons`, `Laureates`, `PleaDistributor`, `PleaLaunch`, and `PleaHook`. Only `CabalGate` changes (runtime grows from 20,028 to 20,075 bytes, still below 24,576).

## Changes in the second revision (follow-up review of the wall guard)

1. **Wall guard still adopted a seller-chosen price limit reached through an empty pool — `356e430a…560b2ac` (high, fixed; settles `07e31e69…c082e6`).** Reproduced exactly as reported, in both currency orientations with the Sepolia timings: after the Cabal's death a 50,000,000 PLEA exact-in sell with the seller's own `sqrtPriceLimitX96` one tick spacing inside the usable range (`minTick + 60` with PLEA token0, `maxTick − 60` with PLEA token1) emptied the market band's IMD, ran through the liquidity-free region to that limit, and `afterSwap` moved `wallGuardTick` from −120721 to −887160 (PLEA token0) and from 120721 to 887161 (PLEA token1) with `poolManager.getLiquidity(poolId()) == 0` at the pool's tick. The previous fix only excluded the two extreme ticks the router's default limit stops at, so any interior limit passed it; `rebalance()` then reverted `InvalidLiquidity` as in the first report. Fix in `src/PleaHook.sol`, `_updateWallGuard`: an observed tick is adopted only when it is strictly inside the usable range **and** the pool has active liquidity at it (`poolManager.getLiquidity(poolId()) != 0`). A tick the pool sits at with no active liquidity is where a swap stopped for lack of IMD, whatever limit the seller chose, not a price PLEA traded at. The check lives in `_updateWallGuard` so it covers both callers, `afterSwap` and `_rebalance`, rather than only the swap path; the time-paced return toward `refTick` runs unchanged either way. A sell that stops inside a band (the market band or the wall) has active liquidity at its final tick and really sold PLEA down to that price, so the guard still drops to it at once as POOL4's floor does; driving the price through the wall's liquidity to a ruinous tick is impossible, since the PLEA needed grows as 1/√price and exceeds the supply long before the band would overflow a `uint128`. No number changed (`WALL_GUARD_TICKS_PER_DAY`, `MAX_REF_STEP`, the band geometry and the fees are as they were); the swap pays one extra pool-state read, inside `SWAP_RESERVE`. `test/utils/Mocks.sol` gained `BuyRouter.sellExactInLimit`, an exact-in sell with the seller's own price limit, as a plain v4 router allows. Tests in `test/Hook.t.sol`, run in both orientations (`HookTest`, `HookPleaIsZeroTest`): `test_wallGuardIgnoresASellerChosenLimitReachedThroughAnEmptyPool` replays the reviewer's sequence with the interior limit, asserts the pool stopped at that tick with zero active liquidity, that the guard did not move, and that after a 50 IMD buy an hour later, `fundWall(10e18)` twelve hours later and `rebalance()`, the wall's near edge is within 1,000 ticks below spot; it failed on the accepted code with `-887160 != -120721`. `test_wallGuardStillDropsAtOnceToASellerChosenLimitInsideTheBand` holds a pump for 30 days so the guard catches up, then sells with a limit 600 ticks below spot inside the market band and checks the guard followed the traded price at once (it passes on both the old and the new code, pinning the behaviour the fix must keep). Transient state worth knowing: while the pool still sits at an interior drained tick, before any buy brings spot back, `rebalance()` reverts `InvalidLiquidity` because `_rebalance` places the band against spot itself; this is the state the pool is in only until the next buy, nothing is lost or parked, and it is unchanged accepted behaviour, so it was left alone.

## Imported audit findings

The central behavior of all four reports reproduces as described below. Three describe explicitly retained launch behavior rather than unauthorized access in the required atomic deployment. The fourth report's literal hook mask does not reproduce: the code requires `0x28CC`, not the reported `0x2ACC`.

1. **Gas documentation — `9ebcf1ccd8ebfd76bc87ad4540cbef0f664b0e27dec12ac3bb1c0bab095ca400`.** Before any executable-source change, `forge test --match-path test/LaunchManifest.t.sol -vv` reported 17,190,214 gas and a margin of −412,998 against 16,777,216, exactly reproducing the report. The README's “about 21k” statement was incorrect and is corrected. The manifest test still reports the margin without asserting the mainnet cap, per the Sepolia brief. Its measured region includes copying the test's stored creation codes into the factory call and excludes transaction intrinsic/calldata gas, so it is not an on-chain receipt or a guarantee of a final transaction's gas. The deployment service must simulate its actual transaction on the target chain. No transaction was broadcast to test public-node admission.
2. **Permanent allowlist — `3094c361c858945196064f06de7544b8497ec5827d0a8d4ab37aa98b8f4a314c`.** `TokenTest.test_walletsCannotSendWhileTheCabalWatches` and `test_distributorAndAllowlistMaySend` exercise an ordinary holder's transfer restriction, an unauthorized allowlist call, and the owner's successful exemption followed by a transfer. An exempt sender can bypass this hook through another venue; direct transfers to this PoolManager or gate still have their separate checks. This owner power is explicitly accepted, so it remains and the README now discloses it.
3. **Deployment-block registration window — `9a2184dc0a6a9b041ff7e3306be42b1268be3c5abdb3f644440e7d20404cb748`.** `RehearsalManifestTest.test_separateTransactionsLeaveTheAcceptedDeploymentBlockWindow` registers an attacker between deployment calls and makes the legitimate `PleaLaunch` constructor revert `AlreadySet`. The later-block rejection and legitimate rehearsal fallback also pass. The brief explicitly requires accepting either the transient deployment flag or deployment block, so both checks remain. `AtomicManifestTest` rehearses the required uninterrupted six-contract factory call. `script/Deploy.s.sol` is only a local simulation helper; its individual broadcast transactions are unsuitable for this public launch.
4. **Explicit launch exceptions — `e3aaf7b610329f3e6b83590bd5f04043145379d905f9478918d02b2c309c53b1`.** `PostLaunchHookTest.test_committedHookDeploysLaterAndMintsOnlyTheFixedSplit` confirms zero supply after the six-contract transaction and exactly the fixed split after permissionless CREATE2 deployment of the committed hook code. The imported `PleaLaunch.FLAGS` expression evaluates to `0x28CC`, matching `PleaHook.getHookPermissions`; the audit's `0x2ACC` adds a before-remove-liquidity bit that this hook does not enable and would be rejected. The existing salt miner correctly uses `FLAGS`, so no code change is needed. A second hook deployment is rejected; `TokenTest.test_launcherAndInitAreOneShot` covers duplicate initialization. These steps and the Cabal's temporary transfer restrictions are requested behavior. The distributor's owner-set, one-time Merkle root also remains; claims cannot proceed until the owner sets it, and there is no sweep. The manifest runtime scanner checks all six deployed applications for the forbidden escape opcodes.

`PleaHook` is still absent from the six-entry manifest. Its salt is mined off chain against `PleaLaunch.hookInitCodeHash`; `deployHook` and `seed` remain the explicit post-launch steps. The owner may seed before `START`, and anyone may seed at or after it. The application's fixed oracle configuration and existing ECDSA-first/ERC-1271 signature compatibility are retained rather than adding new administrative powers from the generic examples.
